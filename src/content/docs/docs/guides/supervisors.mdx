A **supervisor** is a long-running agent session (Claude Code by default) that watches a group of quests. It answers the routine prompts your agents stop on, pulls the next quest from its queue when a slot frees up, can complete quests that meet your definition of done, and leaves anything risky flagged for you.

Supervisors are not tied to a project: one supervisor can watch quests from every repository you have, and you can run several supervisors at once -- for example one for feature work and one for nightly chores.

:::note
Before 0.65 each project had its own supervisor toggle in the project header. That UI is gone; supervisors now live on the **Supervisors** board described below, and no supervisor exists until you create one.
:::

## The Supervisors board

Click the **robot** button on the left of the bottom bar, after Sagas (tooltip **Supervisors**), to open the board. The robot is awake while any supervisor is running and shows an orange **!** when a running supervisor has a quest that needs your attention.

The board is a grid with one sheet per supervisor. Each sheet shows:

- Its name, a running/stopped dot and **Start** / **Stop**. While it runs, a speech bubble (**View supervisor chat**) opens its session, and a count shows how many of its quests need attention.
- A gear (**Rules**) that opens its [rules](#rules).
- **Working** -- its active quests, with a `used/ceiling` slot counter when **Pull backlog to active** is on.
- **Up next** -- its numbered queue. Held quests sit at the end, unnumbered and marked **Held**.

At the end of the grid a dashed card offers **+ New supervisor**; you can also **drop a quest here to found one**.

The header holds **Filter quests…**, which filters the whole board: it narrows the Unassigned list and dims non-matching rows on sheets. Esc clears the filter; a second Esc closes the board.

Next to it, **Unassigned** (with a count) opens the pool of active and backlog quests with no supervisor, grouped by project, on the left. It is hidden by default, since most handovers happen from the Quest Log, and the board remembers whether you left it open.

### Assigning quests

Any of these hands a quest to a supervisor:

- **Drag** a quest card from Unassigned (or from another sheet) onto a sheet. Where you drop it in **Up next** sets its position in the queue; an amber line shows exactly where it will land.
- **Drag a project header** from Unassigned onto a sheet to hand over every open quest in that project at once. Quests created later are not assigned automatically unless the project has a default supervisor.
- Right-click a quest card in the quest log and pick a supervisor under **Add to Supervisor**. The menu is disabled until a supervisor exists.
- Pick a **Supervisor** in the Create Quest or Edit Quest dialog.
- Set a **Default supervisor** in [Project Settings](/get-sidequest/docs/getting-started/first-workspace/#project-settings). New quests in that project -- from the dialog, the CLI and agent-created quests -- start with it; you can still change it per quest. Changing the default never moves existing quests.
- Wire a Saga's Quest step to a supervisor card (see [Sagas and supervisors](#sagas-and-supervisors)).
- From a terminal or another agent, `sidequest quest assign`. See the [CLI reference](/get-sidequest/docs/advanced/cli/).

A quest belongs to at most one supervisor, and Shell quests can't have one. To take a quest back, hover a row on the sheet and click **Unassign**, drag it back to Unassigned, choose **None** under **Add to Supervisor**, or set its supervisor to **None** in Edit Quest. Unsupervised quests behave exactly as before -- they run, but nobody answers their prompts for you.

**Move to Backlog** on a supervised quest holds it: it stays in **Up next**, marked **Held**, and is not pulled again until you edit or start it. `sidequest quest backlog` does the same from a terminal; `sidequest quest hold` holds a quest already in the backlog, and `release` lets it be pulled again.

Quest cards show a small robot when they are supervised; click it to open the board scrolled to that quest.

## What a supervisor does

<Mermaid chart={`graph TD
  S[Supervisor session] -->|each pass| L[Read each quest's terminal]
  L -->|prompt waiting| A[Answer it]
  L -->|risky action| E[Leave flagged: needs attention]
  L -->|meets close criteria| C[Complete quest]
  W[Sidequest watcher] -->|slot free| P[Pull top of queue to Active]
  W -->|new work, supervisor running| N[Nudge it to run a pass now]
  N --> S
  W -->|work waiting & auto-start| S`} />

On each pass the supervisor reads the terminal of each of its active quests and:

- **Answers prompts** -- permission requests, "(Recommended)" choices, yes/no confirmations and small design questions, so quests keep moving. It then clears the quest's attention flag.
- **Escalates** -- destructive git operations, merges to main, deleting worktrees, anything involving secrets, external side effects or notable cost are left alone with the quest marked **Needs attention**, and the reason is logged in the supervisor's own terminal.
- **Closes quests** -- when **Close completed quests** is on and a quest meets your criteria, the supervisor completes it (it may check `gh pr list` and CI first).
- **Watches child supervisors** -- see [Reporting lines](#reporting-lines).
- **Keeps notes** -- reads its notes file at the start of each loop and appends what it handled and learned at the end, so a restart or context compaction does not make it redo work.
- Prints a status table of its quests, what each is doing and its last action.

It does all of this through the `sidequest` CLI, which from a supervisor's session reaches only its own group's quests and the supervisors reporting to it.

A Claude Code supervisor waits about 30 seconds between passes. Other agents end their turn after each pass, and Sidequest prompts them to run the next one once they have sat idle for a minute.

When quests join a running supervisor's group, Sidequest nudges it within seconds so it runs a pass right away. It never sends a nudge on top of text you've typed into the supervisor's prompt.

Sidequest itself, not the agent, handles the queue: while a supervisor is running and **Pull backlog to active** is on, the top quest in **Up next** is promoted whenever fewer than **ceiling** of its quests are active, and its session is launched in the background.

To see the supervisor think, click the speech bubble on its sheet: a full-screen view of its session opens. Press Esc to return to the board. From a terminal, `sidequest supervisor status`, `read` and `send` watch and answer it (see the [CLI reference](/get-sidequest/docs/advanced/cli/)).

## Rules

Click the gear on a sheet to open its rules. The grid fades out and that supervisor's rules fill the board; **All supervisors** or Esc takes you back. Most options are baked into the session's prompt, so they are locked while the supervisor is running; stop it, change them, and start it again.

| Option | Default | Description |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| **Name** | "New supervisor" | Click the name on the sheet or in the editor to edit it inline |
| **Reports to** | None | Another supervisor that watches this one's session (editable while running) |
| **Agent** | Claude Code | Claude Code, Codex CLI, Copilot CLI or OpenCode. Changing it resets **Model** to Auto |
| **Model** | Auto (agent default) | A model from the chosen agent's list, a recently used one, or any model id. Takes effect on the next start |
| **Auto-answer active quests** | On | Answer prompts in supervised quests |
| **Auto-start when work is waiting** | Off | Start this supervisor by itself whenever it has work (editable while running) |
| **Pull backlog to active** | On | Promote queued quests as slots free up, up to the **ceiling** (1--10, default 3) |
| **Close completed quests** | Off | Complete quests that meet the criteria you describe in the text box |
| **Extended prompt** | Off | Extra instructions appended to the supervisor's prompt |
| **Delete supervisor** | | At the bottom of the editor. The confirmation lists what still uses it: projects that default to it (the default is cleared), open quests (unassigned; queued ones stop being pulled), supervisors reporting to it (they become top-level) and Sagas (quests wired to it run unsupervised) |

See [Supervisor Customization](/get-sidequest/docs/advanced/supervisor-customization/) for how the prompt is assembled, what each agent supports, and examples of close criteria and extended prompts.

You can also manage supervisors from a terminal with `sidequest supervisor`; see the [CLI reference](/get-sidequest/docs/advanced/cli/).

## Starting and stopping

Click **Start** on a sheet to launch the supervisor; **Stop** ends its session. Start fails with a message if the supervisor's agent isn't installed or isn't at the path set in Settings.

Each supervisor owns one session. A Claude Code supervisor resumes that conversation on **Start**, so a restarted supervisor still knows what it did; other agents start a new conversation each time. Either way the brief is sent again, so rule changes made while it was stopped take effect, and its notes file carries over (see [Supervisor Customization](/get-sidequest/docs/advanced/supervisor-customization/#the-notes-file)).

With **Auto-start when work is waiting** on, Sidequest starts the supervisor by itself as soon as it has something to do: an active quest, a queued quest (if pulling is enabled) or a running child supervisor. A manual **Stop** suppresses auto-start until the supervisor's work count drops to zero or Sidequest restarts, so a stopped supervisor stays stopped. If five automatic starts in a row fail, Sidequest gives up and asks you to start it by hand to see the error.

A running supervisor **stops by itself** once all of its quests are completed or unassigned -- unless it fills a slot in a Saga whose trigger is on, or is the parent of another supervisor, in which case it stays up.

## Reporting lines

Set **Reports to** on a supervisor and the chosen parent watches that supervisor's *own* session the way the supervisor watches its quests: it reads the terminal, answers prompts, and counts the child's quests in its status table (reporting "down with N quests waiting" when a child with work is not running). The parent never touches the child's quests directly and never starts or stops the child.

A supervisor has at most one parent, cannot report to itself, and cannot be part of a loop; the picker hides any choice that would create one. Sheets show **↳ reports to Name** under the title.

Use this to keep a "lead" supervisor running cheaply on Sonnet that unblocks several specialised ones, or to give a nightly chores supervisor someone to answer it at 3 a.m.

## Sagas and supervisors

In a [Saga](/get-sidequest/docs/guides/sagas/), a supervisor appears as a card. Wire it to a Quest step and the quest that step creates is handed to it. The card shows what the supervisor tends and its rules; you can change them there, with a warning that the change applies everywhere it works, not only in that Saga. To make a new one, pick **+ New supervisor** on the card. Which supervisor fills a card is kept on your machine, not shared with the Saga.

## Tips

- **One supervisor per kind of work.** Keep a cheap Sonnet supervisor for chores and a careful Opus one for feature branches; the ceiling on each controls how much runs in parallel.
- **Order the queue deliberately.** The top of **Up next** is pulled first; drag rows to reorder them.
- **Watch the orange badge.** The robot's **!** means a supervisor chose not to act on something. Open the quest and answer it yourself.
- **Supervisors run unattended with permissions bypassed.** They have full shell access; the `sidequest` CLI keeps them to their own group, but that guards against slips, not misuse. Give them close criteria you would be happy to have applied unattended.
